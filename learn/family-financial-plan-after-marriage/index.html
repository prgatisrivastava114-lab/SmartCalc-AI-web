💍 Just Married? Your Family’s First Financial Plan

💍 नई शादी? अपने परिवार की पहली फ़ाइनेंशियल योजना

7 min read · English + हिन्दी · by My Financial Plan · single & double income families दोनों के लिए

One salary or two — marriage turns two money lives into one shared future. Whether only the husband earns (still India’s most common pattern) or both partners earn, the first year of marriage is when a family’s money story is written. Do these five steps in your first 90 days.

Why the first year sets your money future

  • Habits set now — budget, savings day, insurance — run on autopilot for decades.
  • Every goal (home, children, retirement) gets more compounding years when started this year.
  • ESTIMATE (general guidance): starting goal SIPs at 28 instead of 35 can roughly halve the monthly amount needed for the same goal.

Step 1 — Put the family’s money on one honest sheet

Case A — husband is the only earning member: list the one take-home salary, all expenses and EMIs. The entire plan runs on this single income — which is why Steps 3–4 below are non-negotiable for you.

Case B — both partners earn: list both take-home salaries, shared and personal spends, EMIs. No hiding, no judging — the plan starts from your combined surplus.

Homemaker’s note: running the home is unpaid full-time work. The plan belongs to both partners — so both must sit in this exercise.

Step 2 — Follow the 50-30-20 family budget

50% needs (rent, food, EMIs) · 30% wants (travel, dining) · 20% savings + insurance — savings must leave on salary day, before lifestyle grows.

Example family (assumption figures)Monthly incomeNeeds 50%Wants 30%Savings 20%
Case A: husband earns ₹60,000₹60,000₹30,000₹18,000₹12,000
Case B: ₹50,000 + ₹30,000₹80,000₹40,000₹24,000₹16,000
Tight budget on one income? Start savings at 10% and step up every 6 months — consistency beats size.

Step 3 — Emergency fund before anything else

  • Rule: 6 months of household expenses in a separate liquid/sweep-in option. Example: ₹40,000/month expenses → ₹2,40,000 target.
  • Case A (single income): if the one salary pauses, everything pauses — aim closer to 9 months (ESTIMATE, general guidance).
  • Case B (dual income): 6 months works — a job loss hurts but doesn’t stop the household.

Step 4 — Protect first, invest later (insurance)

Case A — husband the sole earner:

  • His term life cover is the family’s lifeline — rule of thumb: 10–15× annual income + outstanding loans (ESTIMATE).
  • Family floater health cover for husband + wife (+ children later) — never rely only on office group policy; a job change leaves a gap.
A homemaker’s value is real: replacing her unpaid work (childcare, home management) costs real money — the Human Life Value method counts this too.

Case B — both earn: each partner needs their own term cover, sized to their income share — not just the husband’s. One family floater + adequate individual health covers.

Money talks: 3 simple rules that save marriages

  • One 30-minute “money date” every month — review, don’t blame.
  • Small spends are personal; big spends (fix a number, e.g. ₹5,000+) are discussed.
  • Both partners must know every account, nominee and document location — even if only one earns.

यही lesson हिन्दी में 🇮🇳

शादी के बाद का पहला साल ही परिवार की आर्थिक कहानी लिखता है — चाहे घर में सिर्फ़ पति कमाते हों या दोनों। पहले 90 दिनों में ये 5 क़दम उठाएँ:

क़दम 1: पूरे परिवार की आय और ख़र्च एक ही sheet पर लिखिए। अकेली कमाई है तो पूरा प्लान उसी एक salary पर चलेगा — इसलिए क़दम 3-4 आपके लिए सबसे ज़रूरी। घर संभालना भी full-time काम है — प्लान दोनों का है।
क़दम 2 (50-30-20 budget): 50% ज़रूरतें, 30% शौक, 20% बचत + insurance — बचत salary के दिन ही निकल जाए। उदा: ₹60,000 (एक आय) → ₹12,000 बचत; ₹80,000 (दो आय) → ₹16,000। तंग बजट हो तो 10% से शुरू करके हर 6 महीने बढ़ाएँ।
क़दम 3 (emergency fund): 6 महीने के ख़र्च जितनी राशि अलग रखिए (उदा: ₹40,000/माह ख़र्च → ₹2,40,000)। अकेली कमाई के घर में 9 महीने तक का लक्ष्य बेहतर (अनुमान) — कमाई रुकी तो सब रुकता है।
क़दम 4 (पहले सुरक्षा): अकेले कमाने वाले husband का term cover (अनुमान: सालाना आय का 10–15 गुना + loans) परिवार की lifeline है + पूरे परिवार का health cover। दोनों कमाते हैं तो दोनों का अलग term cover ज़रूरी — सिर्फ़ पति का नहीं। गृहिणी के unpaid काम की भी कीमत होती है।
क़दम 5 (हर goal की अलग SIP): घर, बच्चों की पढ़ाई-शादी, retirement — SIP salary वाले दिन auto कर दीजिए। एक आय में SIP छोटी होगी, कोई बात नहीं — समय पर शुरू ₹5,000 की SIP, 10 साल देर से शुरू ₹15,000 से आगे निकल जाती है (अनुमान)।
महीने में एक बार 30 मिनट की “money meeting” — दोनों को हर account और nominee पता हो, भले एक ही कमाता हो।

Frequently asked questions

My wife is a homemaker — is this plan still for us?

Absolutely. The plan belongs to the family, not the salary. She should know every account and nominee, join the monthly money date, and her future security — your adequate term cover and some savings in her name — is a core part of the plan.

We both earn, but unequally — should we split 50-50?

Plan from combined income; contribution ratios may differ. What matters is that both names are on the plan.

Must all our bank accounts be joint?

Not required. One joint account for household + goals, plus individual accounts, works well for most couples — what matters is shared visibility.

We already have loans — invest or prepay first?

Emergency fund + insurance come first. Then compare: high-interest debt (credit cards, personal loans) usually wins the prepay race before extra investing.

No — this is an educational framework with indicative estimates only. Consult a registered adviser for personal advice.
