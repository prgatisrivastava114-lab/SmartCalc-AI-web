Income Tax Calculator — New vs Old Regime

Enter your income once and this calculator works out your tax under both the new and the old regime for FY 2026-27, then tells you which one leaves more money in your hand. Under the new regime, taxable income up to ₹12,00,000 attracts no tax because of the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 — and for a salaried person the ₹75,000 standard deduction pushes that to roughly ₹12.75 lakh of gross salary.

Which regime should you actually choose?

Most "income tax calculators" make you pick a regime first. That is backwards — choosing the regime is the decision worth getting right, and it is worth tens of thousands of rupees a year. This tool computes both at once and shows you the gap, so the answer is a number rather than an opinion.

The new regime is the default — you have to opt out

Since FY 2023-24 the new regime applies automatically unless you actively choose the old one. That matters: if you have a home loan, pay rent, or invest heavily under 80C, staying silent can cost you money. The reverse is also true — many people keep buying tax-saving products out of habit when the new regime would already have left them better off.

What the new regime gives you

Lower rates across seven bands, a ₹75,000 standard deduction for salary and pension income, and a Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 that wipes out tax entirely where taxable income does not exceed ₹12 lakh. What it takes away is almost every deduction: 80C, 80D, HRA under Section 10(13A), and home-loan interest on a self-occupied property are all unavailable.

What the old regime still gives you

A far wider set of deductions — ₹1.5 lakh under 80C, health insurance under 80D, HRA, the additional ₹50,000 for NPS under 80CCD(1B), and up to ₹2 lakh of home-loan interest. The price is higher slab rates and a basic exemption of only ₹2.5 lakh. The old regime wins only when your genuine deductions are large; the calculator above prints the exact break-even figure for your income.

One deduction survives in both

Your employer’s contribution to NPS under Section 80CCD(2) — up to 14% of basic plus DA — is deductible under both regimes. For a new-regime taxpayer it is effectively the only major lever left, which is why asking HR to restructure part of your CTC into employer NPS is often the single highest-value tax move available. Our NPS calculator shows what that contribution grows into.

How this calculator works

FormulaTax = slab tax on taxable income − Section 87A rebate + surcharge + 4% cess

Taxable income is gross income minus the standard deduction (₹75,000 new regime, ₹50,000 old regime, salary and pension only) and, in the old regime, minus your eligible Chapter VI-A deductions. Slab tax is charged band by band — not at one flat rate on the whole amount. The Section 87A rebate is applied after slab tax, and health and education cess of 4% is applied last, on tax plus surcharge.

Worked example

A salaried person in Varanasi earning ₹15,00,000 a year, with ₹2,00,000 of genuine old-regime deductions (₹1.5 lakh 80C plus ₹50,000 health insurance):

To make the old regime competitive at ₹15 lakh, this person would need about ₹5.44 lakh of real deductions — which usually means a large home-loan interest component on top of a fully used 80C. That is the honest test: not "can I claim deductions", but "do I actually have enough of them".

Limitations — what this tool does not do

  • Capital gains, lottery, crypto and other income taxed at special rates are not included — the Section 87A rebate does not apply to those, and mixing them in would make the answer wrong.
  • Surcharge marginal relief near the ₹50 lakh, ₹1 crore and ₹2 crore thresholds is not modelled, so results just above those lines are slightly overstated.
  • It assumes you are a resident individual. NRIs, HUFs, firms and companies are taxed differently and cannot claim the Section 87A rebate.
  • It does not check whether your claimed deductions are actually allowable — it takes the figure you type at face value.
  • Relief under Section 89 for salary arrears, and foreign tax credits, are outside its scope.

Frequently asked questions

Is income up to ₹12 lakh really tax-free under the new regime?
Up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income, yes — the Section 87A rebate of up to ₹60,000 reduces the liability to nil for resident individuals. For a salaried person the ₹75,000 standard deduction sits on top, so gross salary of about ₹12.75 lakh can end up with zero tax. Note the test is taxable income, not CTC, and the rebate is not available to NRIs or against income taxed at special rates.
What happens if I earn slightly more than ₹12 lakh?
Marginal relief protects you. Without it, earning one rupee over ₹12 lakh would create a large tax bill. The law caps the tax at the amount by which your income exceeds ₹12 lakh, so the liability rises gradually rather than jumping off a cliff. This calculator applies that relief.
Can I switch between the old and new regime every year?
A salaried person without business income can generally choose afresh each year when filing. Someone with business or professional income has far less flexibility — once they opt out of the new regime they can usually return to it only once. If you have business income, take professional advice before switching.
Which regime is better for me?
It depends entirely on how large your real deductions are. The calculator above prints the break-even figure for your income — the amount of old-regime deductions you would need for the old regime to win. If your actual deductions are below that number, the new regime is cheaper.
Does the old regime still exist for FY 2026-27?
Yes. The new regime is the default, but the old regime remains available as an opt-in choice and still suits people with substantial home-loan interest, HRA and 80C claims.
Is HRA available under the new regime?
No. The HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is an old-regime benefit only. If you pay significant rent, work out the exemption with our HRA exemption calculator first — it is often the single largest item that tips the comparison toward the old regime.
