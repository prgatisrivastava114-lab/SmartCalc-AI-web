About this calculator

Present Value (PV) is the mirror image of compounding: it tells you what a future amount is worth in today's money. It is the single most under-used idea in Indian family finance — and the reason most people under-plan their goals.

The present value formula

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ. Discount ₹1 crore at 8% for 20 years and you get roughly ₹21 lakh — meaning ₹1 crore in 2046 buys what ₹21 lakh buys today. That is why "one crore" goals need a second look.

Two different rates — don't mix them

To estimate a goal's future cost (child education, wedding), inflate today's cost by inflation (6–8%). To discount a future value back to today, use your expected investment return. This calculator works for both directions.

Where families use PV

Child education: today's MBBS cost inflated to admission year. Retirement: your future monthly expense in today's terms — then our Retirement calculator converts it into a corpus. Insurance: cover = future family expenses discounted to today.

How this calculator works

This calculator works out what a future amount is worth in today's money. Here is exactly what it assumes, the equation it runs, and what it leaves out.

STEP 1What does this calculator assume?

A single discount rate applied over the whole period, compounded annually.

STEP 2What formula does it use?

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)n.

STEP 3What does this calculator not include?

The discount rate you enter is treated as a nominal rate. Enter a real rate (roughly your expected return minus inflation) if you want the answer expressed in today's purchasing power. Tax is not modelled.

Frequently asked questions

What is the present value formula?

PV = FV ÷ (1 + r)^n — the future amount divided by (1 + rate) raised to the years. It answers: what is that future money worth right now?

Present value vs future value?

They are the same equation seen from opposite ends. FV grows today's money forward; PV shrinks future money back to today.

Which rate should I use — inflation or investment return?

To price a future goal (e.g., education cost 15 years away) use inflation (~6–8%). To judge a future payout (e.g., a policy maturity) use your alternative investment return.

₹1 crore in 20 years — worth what today?

At 6% inflation ≈ ₹31 lakh; at 8% ≈ ₹21 lakh. Try changing the rate above and watch how ruthlessly time shrinks money.
