About this calculator

Got a bonus, an FD maturing, an old insurance payout or accumulated savings? A lumpsum investment puts the entire amount to work from day one. This free lumpsum calculator shows exactly what compounding does to that money over your chosen horizon.

Compounding and the Rule of 72

Divide 72 by your expected return — that's approximately how many years your money takes to double. At 12%, money doubles roughly every 6 years: ₹5 lakh becomes ₹10 lakh, then ₹20 lakh, then ₹40 lakh if you simply stay invested.

When does lumpsum beat SIP?

When money is already available and your horizon is 7+ years, lumpsum historically wins because all of it compounds from day one. SIP wins for salary-based investing and for discipline. Many families do both: lumpsum for windfalls, SIP for income.

Nervous about investing everything at once?

Park the amount in a liquid/debt fund and set an STP (Systematic Transfer Plan) into equity over 6–12 months. You reduce timing risk while your money still earns instead of sleeping in a savings account.

Frequently asked questions

Lumpsum or SIP — which is better?

Lumpsum works best when money is already available and you can stay invested 7+ years. SIP is better for investing from monthly income and for rupee-cost averaging.

How fast will my money double?

Rule of 72: divide 72 by your annual return. At 12%, money doubles roughly every 6 years; at 8%, every 9 years.

Is it risky to invest a large amount at once?

There is timing risk. If worried, park the money in a liquid fund and use an STP into equity over 6–12 months — a calmer middle path.

How are lumpsum gains taxed?

Equity funds: 12.5% long-term capital gains tax on gains above ₹1.25 lakh/year after 12 months of holding. Debt fund gains are taxed at your income slab.
