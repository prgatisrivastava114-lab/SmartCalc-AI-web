About this calculator

Future Value (FV) answers the question every saver has: "If I keep this money invested, what will it become?" This free future value calculator goes beyond basic FV — it also includes the yearly additions most families actually make.

The future value formula

FV = PV × (1 + r)ⁿ for the base amount, plus each yearly addition compounding for its remaining years. A 10% return over 15 years turns ₹2 lakh + ₹60k/year into roughly 4× the invested amount — growth quietly overtakes deposits.

What return should I assume?

Equity mutual funds: 10–12% for long horizons. Debt/FD: 6–7%. PPF: 7.1%. Always plan with a conservative number — a plan that works at 10% and wins at 12% is a safe plan.

Nominal vs real (inflation-adjusted)

This calculator shows nominal value. At 6% inflation, ₹1 crore after 20 years buys what ~₹31 lakh buys today. Use our Present Value calculator for that reverse view — and plan goals in future rupees, not today's.

How this calculator works

This calculator works out what today's savings plus yearly additions become. Here is exactly what it assumes, the equation it runs, and what it leaves out.

STEP 1What does this calculator assume?

Annual compounding. Your yearly addition is applied at the end of each year (an ordinary annuity), not at the start.

STEP 2What formula does it use?

FV = P × (1 + r)n + A × [ (1 + r)n − 1 ] ÷ r, where P is current savings and A the yearly addition.

STEP 3What does this calculator not include?

The result is a nominal figure — it ignores inflation, so it does not tell you what that money will actually buy. Tax and expense ratio are excluded, and the yearly addition is assumed never to change.

STEP 4What is a worked example?

Frequently asked questions

What is the future value formula?

FV = PV × (1 + r)^n, where PV is today's amount, r the annual return and n the years. For yearly additions, each addition compounds separately for its remaining years — our calculator sums it all.

How is this different from the Lumpsum calculator?

Lumpsum assumes one single investment. Future Value also counts yearly additions (like annual bonuses or top-ups) — closer to how real families save.

Does future value account for inflation?

No — FV is in future (nominal) rupees. To know what that amount is worth in today's purchasing power, divide by inflation or use our Present Value calculator.

What is a realistic long-term return in India?

For planning: 10–12% diversified equity, 6–7% debt/FD, 7.1% PPF, 8.2% SSY. Never plan essentials on the best case.
