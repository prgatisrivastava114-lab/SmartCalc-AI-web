Gratuity Calculator

For an employee covered by the Payment of Gratuity Act, gratuity is last drawn basic plus DA × 15/26 × completed years of service. It becomes payable after five years of continuous service, and for private-sector employees it is tax-exempt up to a lifetime ceiling of ₹20 lakh under Section 10(10).

What gratuity are you owed?

Gratuity is money your employer owes you by law, and it is routinely miscalculated — usually by dividing by 30 instead of 26, or by using total CTC instead of basic plus DA. Both errors change the answer materially. This calculator uses the statutory formula and also shows the tax-exempt and taxable split.

Why 15 and why 26

The Act promises 15 days of wages for every completed year of service, and it treats a working month as 26 days rather than 30 — one month less four Sundays. So the multiplier is 15/26, roughly 0.577 of a month’s pay per year served. Dividing by 30 instead of 26 understates gratuity by about 13%, and it is the most common mistake in payroll spreadsheets.

Wages means basic plus DA — not CTC

Only basic salary and dearness allowance enter the formula. HRA, LTA, bonus, performance pay and employer PF do not. This is why employees with a low basic and a large allowance structure receive surprisingly small gratuity. The draft labour codes propose requiring wages to be at least half of total remuneration, which would change this, but the position above reflects the Act as it stands.

The five-year rule and its exceptions

Gratuity becomes payable after five years of continuous service with the same employer. The requirement is waived where service ends because of death or disablement. Some courts have accepted four years and 240 days as qualifying in certain establishments — if you are close to the line, it is worth asking rather than assuming you do not qualify.

Rounding, and when the employer must pay

Any part of a final year exceeding six months counts as a full year; less than six months is dropped. Gratuity is due within 30 days of the separation date, and delay attracts simple interest. If your employer disputes the amount, the arithmetic above is the statutory minimum — they may pay more, but not less.

How this calculator works

FormulaGratuity = (Last drawn basic + DA) × 15 ÷ 26 × completed years of service

For employees not covered by the Act, the comparable figure is half a month’s average salary — based on the last ten months — for each completed year, with no rounding up of part years. The tax exemption is then the least of the actual gratuity received, the formula amount, and the ₹20 lakh lifetime ceiling.

Limitations — what this tool does not do

  • It calculates the statutory minimum. Employers may pay more under contract or policy, and that excess is taxable above the exemption.
  • The ₹20 lakh ceiling is a lifetime limit across all employers. If you have received exempt gratuity before, your remaining headroom is lower than this tool assumes.
  • For employees not covered by the Act, the exemption uses the average of the last ten months’ salary; this tool uses your last drawn figure as an approximation.
  • Gratuity received while still in service, rather than on exit, is fully taxable — the tool assumes payment on separation.
  • Pending labour-code changes could alter eligibility for fixed-term employees and the definition of wages.

Frequently asked questions

Is gratuity taxable?
For government employees, no — it is fully exempt. For private-sector employees covered by the Act, it is exempt up to the least of the actual amount received, the 15/26 formula amount, and ₹20 lakh across your lifetime. Anything above that is added to salary income and taxed at your slab rate.
Do I get gratuity if I resign before five years?
Generally no. Five years of continuous service is the statutory threshold, and it is waived only where service ends due to death or disablement. Some establishments recognise four years and 240 days — worth checking with HR rather than assuming.
Why divide by 26 and not 30?
Because the Act defines a month as 26 working days for this purpose — a calendar month less four Sundays. Using 30 understates your gratuity by roughly 13%, which on a long career is a large sum.
Is gratuity calculated on CTC?
No. Only basic salary plus dearness allowance count. A salary structure with a low basic and large allowances produces lower gratuity, which is one reason the basic-to-CTC ratio is worth negotiating.
What if my employer refuses to pay?
Gratuity is a statutory entitlement payable within 30 days of separation, and delay attracts interest. Disputes are raised with the Controlling Authority under the Act. Keep your appointment letter, payslips and relieving letter.
