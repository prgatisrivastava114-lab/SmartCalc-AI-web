About this calculator

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is India's favourite way to build wealth — small monthly amounts into mutual funds, compound interest doing the heavy lifting. This free SIP calculator shows your maturity value instantly: no login, no signup, no ads.

How does SIP compounding work?

Every month's instalment earns returns, and next month's return is calculated on the bigger balance. Over long horizons the growth (interest) portion usually overtakes the money you invested — that is the magic of compounding. Example: a ₹10,000 monthly SIP at 12% for 20 years becomes ~₹99 lakh, of which ~₹75 lakh is pure growth.

Which return % should I assume?

Conservative long-term planning: 10–12% for diversified equity funds, 6–7% for debt funds, 7.1% for PPF. Never plan a family's essentials on best-case returns — use our Plan page for a 3-scenario view.

SIP vs Lumpsum — which wins?

For salaried families SIP wins on discipline and rupee-cost averaging: you buy more units when markets fall. Lumpsum wins only if you already hold cash and can stay invested 7+ years.

How this calculator works

This calculator works out the future value of a fixed monthly SIP. Here is exactly what it assumes, the equation it runs, and what it leaves out.

STEP 1What does this calculator assume?

Returns compound monthly at one-twelfth of the annual rate you enter, and each instalment is invested at the start of the month (a SIP-due). The rate is held constant for the whole period.

STEP 2What formula does it use?

M = P × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ i × (1 + i), where i = annual rate ÷ 12, n = number of months, P = monthly amount.

STEP 3What does this calculator not include?

Expense ratios, exit loads and capital-gains tax are not deducted. Annual step-ups are not modelled — use the Step-Up SIP calculator for that. A real equity fund does not deliver the same return every year, and a bad year early in the period costs more than the same bad year later.

STEP 4What is a worked example?

Frequently asked questions

How is SIP maturity calculated?

Future Value = P × [((1+i)^n − 1)/i] × (1+i), where P is the monthly SIP, i the monthly return rate, and n the months. Our calculator uses the standard SIP formula published by AMFI, the mutual-fund industry body, and SEBI publishes the same method in its investor education material.

Is ₹500 SIP worth starting?

Yes — starting early beats starting big. ₹500/month at 12% becomes ~₹5 lakh in 20 years; waiting 10 years and doing ₹1,000 reaches only ~₹2.3 lakh.

Can I pause or stop a SIP anytime?

Yes, SIPs are fully flexible — pause, reduce or stop from your fund's app anytime with no penalty.

Does SIP beat FD?

Over 10+ year horizons diversified equity SIPs have historically beaten FDs by a wide margin, but with market risk. Short goals (<3 years) suit FD/RD; long goals suit SIP.
