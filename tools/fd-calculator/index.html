About this calculator

Fixed Deposits remain India's default safe investment — but few people compute the actual maturity or compare it with alternatives. This FD calculator uses the same quarterly-compounding method banks use.

FD interest is fully taxable

Bank FD interest adds to your income and is taxed at your slab (up to 30%+). At 7% FD and 30% slab, your real post-tax return is ~4.9% — often below inflation. That is why emergency money suits FDs, long-horizon money does not.

Laddering beats one big FD

Split into 3–5 FDs maturing in different years: better liquidity, better average rates, and you always have one FD close to maturity.

FD vs Debt mutual funds

Debt funds historically return similarly to FDs pre-tax, but taxation now aligns with slabs (post-2023 rules). FD still wins for simplicity and deposit insurance up to ₹5 lakh (DICGC).

How this calculator works

This calculator works out the maturity value of a fixed deposit. Here is exactly what it assumes, the equation it runs, and what it leaves out.

STEP 1What does this calculator assume?

Quarterly compounding — the convention Indian banks use: r = rate ÷ 400 per quarter, over 4 × years quarters.

STEP 2What formula does it use?

M = P × (1 + r ÷ 4)4y.

STEP 3What does this calculator not include?

The maturity figure is before tax. FD interest is taxed at your income-slab rate and TDS may apply, so your credited amount is lower. Premature-withdrawal penalties and any senior-citizen rate differential are not modelled.

STEP 4What is a worked example?

Frequently asked questions

How is FD maturity calculated?

Maturity = P × (1 + r/400)^(4×years) for quarterly compounding — exactly how Indian banks compound FDs.

Is FD interest taxable every year or at maturity?

It accrues yearly for tax (banks deduct TDS above thresholds) — plan ITR even before maturity.

Which is safer: bank FD or corporate FD?

Bank FDs have DICGC insurance of ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank; corporate FDs pay more but carry company default risk — never put emergency money in them.

Why do longer FDs sometimes pay lower rates?

Banks price future rate expectations (inverted curve). Lock the highest rate only for money you won't need, and ladder the rest.
