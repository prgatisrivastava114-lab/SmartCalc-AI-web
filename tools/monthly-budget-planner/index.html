About this calculator

A budget is not about cutting chai — it is about telling every rupee where to go before the month begins. This free monthly budget planner shows your total spend, leftover savings and savings rate in one glance, and scores you against the benchmark advisors use.

What savings rate should an Indian family target?

Under 10%: fragile — one emergency can push you into debt. 10–20%: stable — start goal SIPs. 20–30%: wealth-building zone — financial freedom becomes visible. Above 30%: aggressive savers reach retirement years early.

Budget first, invest second?

Almost — but the correct order is: (1) emergency fund of 6 months' expenses, (2) adequate term + health insurance, (3) goal SIPs. This planner shows whether your monthly surplus can support steps 1–3. Our 50/30/20 calculator shows the ideal split.

The automation trick

Willpower budgets fail; automated budgets work. Set SIPs and insurance premiums on the 2nd of the month — right after salary — and live on the rest. What you never see, you never miss.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good monthly savings rate in India?

20% of take-home is the healthy benchmark; 10–20% is workable if rising; under 10% needs expense surgery. High earners should target 30%+.

How do I track expenses without an app?

This planner + one honest evening with your bank statement. Fill the 8 buckets from last month's actual spending — the number itself usually triggers the fix.

Should I budget with 50/30/20 instead?

50/30/20 gives the ideal NEEDS/WANTS/SAVINGS split; this planner maps your ACTUAL spending. Use both: reality here, target there.

How big should my emergency fund be?

6 months of the 'Monthly spend' shown above, parked in a liquid fund or sweep-in FD. It comes before any other investment.
