The three things that catch people out

1. TDS is deducted on the whole sale price, not your profit

TDS is deducted on the ENTIRE sale price, not on your profit. This is the single most expensive surprise in an NRI property sale. On a large sale that can mean a very substantial sum withheld while your actual tax on the gain is a fraction of it. There is a remedy — Form 13 — but must be applied for before the sale. it cannot be obtained retrospectively.

2. NRO interest is taxed roughly three times harder than a resident's

Banks deduct 30% plus 4% cess on NRO interest, against 10% for a resident. A treaty claim can reduce that — but only if the paperwork is in place before the interest is paid.

3. Your day count decides everything, and both travel days count

Residency is settled by physical presence over the financial year (1 April to 31 March), not by your passport or your visa. If your Indian-source income is above ₹15,00,000, the comfortable 182-day line drops to 120 days.

Who counts as an NRI

NRI. A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is a person resident outside India who is a citizen of India.

PIO. A Person of Indian Origin (PIO) is a person resident outside India who is a citizen of another country (other than Bangladesh or Pakistan) and who was an Indian citizen, or belonged to territory that became part of India after 15 August 1947, or is a child, grandchild or great-grandchild of such a person, or is their foreign-origin spouse. An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholder resident outside India is included.

Important: The FEMA definition above decides which bank accounts you may hold. It is NOT the same test that decides your income tax. Tax residency is decided separately, by counting days in India.

Frequently asked questions

Does my OCI card make me an NRI for tax?

No. An OCI card is about citizenship and entry rights. Indian income tax looks only at how many days you were physically present in India during the financial year, together with your history over the preceding years. Someone with an OCI card can be a tax resident of India, and an Indian citizen can be a non-resident.

Is my foreign salary taxed in India?

If you are a non-resident, or RNOR, your foreign salary is generally outside the Indian tax net. It becomes taxable once you are an ordinarily resident. There is a narrow exception for RNOR: income from a business controlled from India, or a profession set up in India, stays taxable.

Do I have to file an Indian return if tax was already deducted?

Deduction and filing are different things. Tax deducted at source is often more than you actually owe — particularly on NRO interest and property sales — and filing is how you claim the excess back. Whether filing is compulsory depends on your income level, so check your position for the year.
