NRE, NRO or FCNR — which account holds what

Most NRI tax friction traces back to money sitting in the wrong account. The rules are not complicated once laid side by side, and they come straight from the Reserve Bank.

The one-line rule

Money you earn abroad belongs in NRE or FCNR. Money that arises in India belongs in NRO.

Follow that and the tax treatment looks after itself: interest in the first two is exempt and the balances move freely, while the third is taxable and limited. Mix them up and you create avoidable tax and avoidable paperwork.

NRE or FCNR — which of the two?

NRE holds rupees, so you carry the exchange risk but earn rupee interest rates. FCNR holds the foreign currency itself as a term deposit, so there is no rupee exchange risk on the principal. Which suits you depends on whether you expect to spend the money in India or take it back out.

When your status changes

Returning to India

  • NRE accounts must be designated as resident accounts, or the funds moved to an RFC account, immediately on return to India.
  • FCNR(B) deposits may run to maturity at the contracted rate; on maturity they convert to a resident rupee account or an RFC account.
  • NRO accounts may be designated as resident accounts on return to India for an uncertain period.

Leaving India

  • When a resident becomes a person resident outside India, the existing resident account must be designated as an NRO account.

This step is skipped constantly, in both directions, and it is one of the most common compliance failures in NRI banking. Operating a resident savings account after becoming a non-resident is not a technicality.

Limitations of this page

  • This summarises the main account types for individuals. Other account types exist for specific purposes.
  • Rules for nationals of certain countries differ and may require prior approval.
  • Interest rates are set by banks under regulatory guidelines and are not covered here.
  • Rules change. Check the RBI source linked above for the current text before acting.

Frequently asked questions

Is NRE interest really tax-free?

The RBI states that income earned in NRE and FCNR(B) accounts is exempt from income tax. That exemption is tied to your status — once you become an ordinarily resident, the position changes and the accounts must be redesignated.

Can I keep my old resident savings account after moving abroad?

No. When a resident becomes a person resident outside India, the existing resident account should be designated as an NRO account. Leaving it as a resident account is a compliance failure, not a shortcut.

Can my parents in India operate my NRI account?

An NRE or NRO account can be operated by a resident under a power of attorney, but only within limits — broadly, local payments and remittance to the account holder. It is not open-ended access.

What happens to my FCNR deposit if I move back to India?

It may be allowed to run to maturity at the contracted rate if you wish. On maturity it converts to a resident rupee account or an RFC account, at your option, if eligible.
