NRI property sale — TDS calculator

When an NRI sells property in India, tax is withheld on the entire sale price, not on the profit. This works out the gap between what gets withheld and what you actually owe — which is the money you will be waiting to get back.

About this calculator

A resident selling property has 1% withheld. An NRI has a double-digit percentage withheld — and it comes off the whole sale price rather than the profit. That difference is why NRI sellers so often find a large sum sitting with the tax department for months.

This tool shows three numbers: what the buyer is obliged to withhold, what you will actually owe once the gain is computed, and the difference between them. That third number is the point of the exercise.

How this calculator works

  1. It works out your gain. Sale price, less what you paid, less improvement costs, less selling expenses.
  2. It checks the holding period. More than 24 months is long-term. 24 months or less is short-term and taxed far more heavily.
  3. It finds your surcharge band. Surcharge steps up with the sale consideration, so the effective withholding rate rises with the size of the deal.
  4. It applies the withholding to the full sale price — because that is what the rule requires — and the real rate to your taxable gain.
  5. It subtracts one from the other to show the cash you would be waiting on.

Effective withholding rates on a long-term sale

Sale considerationBuild-upEffective rate
Up to ₹50 lakh12.5% + 0% surcharge + 4% cess13.00%
₹50 lakh to ₹1 crore12.5% + 10% surcharge + 4% cess14.30%
Above ₹1 crore12.5% + 15% surcharge + 4% cess14.95%

Surcharge on this category of capital gains is generally described as capped at 15%. Property held for 24 months or less is short-term. TDS is at slab rates plus surcharge and cess — commonly around 30% or more of the full sale price.

The formula

Capital gain = Sale price − Purchase price − Improvements − Selling expenses

TDS withheld = Sale price × effective rate for your band

Tax actually due = Taxable gain × (base rate × (1 + surcharge) × (1 + cess))

Cash locked up = TDS withheld − Tax actually due

Note the asymmetry in the second and third lines: the withholding applies to the sale price, the tax applies to the gain. That asymmetry is the whole problem.

A worked example

Take a flat sold for ₹2,00,00,000, bought years ago for ₹70,00,000, with ₹5,00,000 of improvements and ₹2,00,000 of selling costs, held for eight years.

  • Gain: ₹2,00,00,000 − ₹70,00,000 − ₹5,00,000 − ₹2,00,000 = ₹1,23,00,000
  • Sale is above ₹1 crore, so the effective withholding rate is 14.95% of ₹2,00,00,000 = ₹29,90,000
  • Tax actually due on the gain at the same effective rate: about ₹18,38,850
  • Difference sitting with the department: roughly ₹11,51,150

That gap is not a tax. It is your money, recoverable, but only after you file — unless you obtain Form 13 before the sale.

Lower / Nil Deduction Certificate — the thing most sellers miss

An authorisation from the Income Tax Department telling your buyer to deduct TDS at a lower rate — or nil — based on the tax you will actually owe on the gain, rather than a flat percentage of the whole sale price.

Timing is everything. Must be applied for BEFORE the sale. It cannot be obtained retrospectively. Professional sources commonly advise applying several weeks ahead; reported turnaround varies.

If you miss it: The only remaining route is to claim the excess back by filing an Indian income tax return. Sources describe that refund taking several months to well over a year.

What your buyer has to do

  • The buyer deducts and deposits the TDS, and files the non-resident TDS return.
  • A purchase from an NRI is not the same filing as a purchase from a resident — professional sources stress that the resident form is the wrong one here.
  • The NRI seller needs an Indian PAN; without one, a higher deduction rate applies.
Unconfirmed — do not rely on this. One source reports that a buyer's TAN requirement is replaced by a PAN-based challan from 1 October 2026. We could not corroborate this, so do not plan around it — ask your CA what applies on your completion date.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Figures are indicative. The exact computation depends on documentation the calculator cannot see.
  • The option to pay 20% with indexation on property bought before 23 July 2024 is generally described as available to residents only. This tool does not model an indexation alternative for non-residents.
  • Short-term gains are taxed at slab rates. The tool uses 30% as an indicative figure; your actual slab may differ.
  • Reinvestment exemptions are treated as a simple reduction in the taxable gain. Real exemptions carry conditions, time limits and reinvestment vehicles that must be satisfied.
  • No treaty position is applied. A treaty may alter the outcome.
  • Currency conversion benefits available to some non-resident sellers are not modelled.
  • The tool does not know whether the property is residential or commercial, jointly held, inherited, or subject to a loan — all of which can matter.

Frequently asked questions

Why is tax deducted on the whole sale price instead of my profit?

Because the buyer has no reliable way to verify what you originally paid, what you spent on improvements, or which exemptions you qualify for. The rule therefore puts the obligation on the full consideration as a safeguard, and leaves it to you to reclaim the excess — or to pre-empt it with a lower deduction certificate.

Can I avoid the deduction entirely?

Not by ignoring it. You can reduce it, sometimes to nil, by applying for a lower or nil deduction certificate before the sale, where your computed liability justifies it. What you cannot do is obtain one after the event.

How long does a refund take if I miss the certificate?

Professional sources describe waits ranging from several months to well over a year, depending on when in the year you sell and how quickly the return is processed and verified. That is the cost of skipping the certificate.

Is the long-term rate 12.5% or 20%?

Sources published during 2026 disagree. The majority position is 12.5% without indexation following the Finance Act 2024 change, with the 20%-with-indexation alternative described as available to residents only. A minority of sources still quote 20%. This calculator uses 12.5% and flags the disagreement, because the difference is too large to paper over. Confirm with a chartered accountant before acting.

Do I need an Indian PAN to sell?

Yes, in practical terms. Without a PAN a higher deduction rate applies, and you would have no straightforward way to claim credit for the tax deducted or to receive a refund.
