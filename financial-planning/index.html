How the plan is built

This page follows the same five steps as the MyFinancialPlan app — family, money, goals, protection, plan — and runs the same planning engine behind them. The order is deliberate: you cannot judge whether a goal is affordable until the surplus is known, and goal investing should not start while the emergency reserve and life cover are missing.

The six pillars of the health score

The score is six separate checks. Each pillar is scored out of 100 on its own, and the headline number is their average — so the six rings tell you exactly which pillar is pulling the score down.

A worked example

The plan opens with the household the engine ships as its default: age 32, retiring at 60, ₹85,000 a month against ₹42,000 of living cost, ₹13,70,000 invested across nine asset types, ₹50,00,000 of term cover, and four goals.

Twenty-eight years of 6% inflation turns a ₹40,000 monthly retirement need into ₹2,04,467 a month, which is ₹5,44,16,137 of corpus. The ₹4,00,000 allocated to retirement grows to about ₹96 lakh, leaving roughly ₹4.49 crore to build and a required SIP near ₹16,263 a month for that goal alone.

The number that matters is not the corpus. It is whether the combined SIP across all four goals fits inside the monthly surplus — and when it does not, the plan says so plainly rather than showing a figure nobody can afford.

Assumptions and limitations

Fixed in the engine: the emergency reserve counted as fixed deposits in full plus 30% of mutual funds, a life-cover reference of 15× annual earned income, and a health-cover reference of 10% of that life cover. Chosen by you: growth before retirement and growth after retirement are picked separately on the retirement slide from the same four rungs — Aggressive 14%, Equity 12%, Balanced 10%, Debt 7% — and the pension is paid from the day you retire until the life expectancy you set, 85 by default.

You control: inflation and expected return on every goal, your retirement age, the allocation of existing money to each goal, and any SIP already running.

Not modelled: tax on investments or withdrawals, rental or business income, a spouse's separate finances, medical costs beyond stated living expenses, pension or annuity income, and inheritance. The projection assumes one smooth return every year, which no real market delivers — the order in which good and bad years arrive matters, especially close to retirement.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same plan as the MyFinancialPlan app?

It follows the same five steps the app uses — family, money, goals, protection, plan — and runs the same calculation engine behind them. You get the same financial health score pillars, the same goal maths, the same retirement freedom number and the same every-rupee-has-a-job allocation. The app additionally saves your plan to your account, syncs it across devices and feeds the Partner CRM; this page keeps everything in the browser.

Why five steps instead of one long form?

Because the order matters. You cannot judge whether a goal is affordable until you know the surplus, and you should not be investing for goals at all if the emergency reserve and life cover are missing. The steps follow the same sequence a planner would work through: understand, then protect, then fund.

Is it free, and do I need an account?

Free, with no account. Everything runs in your browser. No figure you enter is sent to a server, which also means nothing is stored — close the tab and your inputs are gone, so download the PDF before you leave.

How is the financial health score calculated?

Six pillars: savings 22, insurance 20, emergency reserve 18, goals 15, investment mix 13 and debt 12. Each is scored against the thresholds shown in the table on this page, so the breakdown tells you exactly which pillar is costing you points rather than giving you one opaque number.

Why does the retirement goal show a corpus in crores when I entered a monthly figure?

Retirement is the only goal whose cost is a monthly amount rather than a one-off. Entering ₹40,000 means ₹40,000 every month from the day you retire until your life expectancy. At 6% inflation over 28 years that ₹40,000 becomes about ₹2.04 lakh a month, and paying it for 25 years from a corpus still earning the Balanced 10% needs roughly ₹3.93 crore. Change the post-retirement growth to Debt 7% and the same pension needs about ₹5.44 crore, because a slower-growing corpus has to be bigger. Every other goal treats the figure you enter as a single future cost.

What does every rupee has a job mean?

It splits your monthly surplus across four jobs — protect, children, retire and wealth — based on what your goals actually demand, and shows whatever is left as flexibility. If the four jobs exceed your surplus, the plan says so rather than quietly showing a SIP you cannot afford.

How do I get the PDF plan?

Finish step five and press Download full plan. Your browser's print dialog opens — choose Save as PDF. The report covers the household, where you stand, all six pillars, every goal, your total financial requirement, the allocation and the assumptions used.
