A wedding planned for 20 years from now will not cost what one costs today. Venues, catering and decoration all rise with inflation, and in most Indian cities faster than it. Planning with today’s estimate is the most common reason families end up borrowing near the date.

The inflation effect is larger than it looks

Over 21 years, even a moderate inflation rate multiplies the cost several times over. ₹15 lakh today becomes roughly ₹51 lakh at 6%. That is a large number, and it is the honest one — the alternative is discovering it later with no time left.

You can change the inflation figure to match what you actually observe in your city. If you are unsure, calculate at two rates and plan for the higher one. Being pleasantly surprised is better than being short.

Starting early changes the monthly figure dramatically

This is the single most useful thing to understand about long-horizon goals. The same wedding costs far less per month if you start when the child is four rather than when they are eighteen.

The reason is compounding. Early instalments have decades to grow, so each rupee you commit does more of the work. Starting late means the contributions themselves have to be larger, because there is almost no growth to help. Change the current age field and watch the SIP figure move — the difference is usually the strongest argument for starting now.

Existing savings and family contributions

If grandparents, relatives or a dedicated gold or FD reserve is earmarked for this, enter the total as existing savings. It grows over the full term like any other investment.

If contributions are expected to come in later rather than now — a bonus, a property sale, a maturity — this calculator cannot model future lump sums. A practical approach is to plan the full SIP now, then reduce it when those amounts actually arrive. Planning for money you expect but do not yet have is how shortfalls happen.

An important note on expectations

This calculator tells you what a wedding of a given cost requires. It does not tell you what to spend, and a larger number is not a better plan. Many families find that deciding the budget first, then checking what it needs monthly, leads to a more comfortable decision than working out what they can afford and spending it.

If the required SIP looks impossible, the honest options are: a longer horizon by starting earlier, a lower budget, or a partial plan funded by a loan at the time. Knowing which of those you are choosing, years in advance, is far better than finding out in the final year.

How this calculator works

This calculator works out the future cost of a daughter's marriage, and the monthly investment needed to fund it. Here is exactly what it assumes, the equation it runs, and what it leaves out.

STEP 1What does this calculator assume?

This calculator uses the MyFinancialPlan core engine. Unless you change them, it assumes 6% inflation and a 12% annual investment return. Any amount you have already allocated to the goal also compounds at that same 12%.

STEP 2What formula does it use?

Future cost = today's cost × (1 + inflation)^years. The plan then compares that future cost with the future value of what you have already allocated, and converts the shortfall into a monthly SIP at the assumed return.

STEP 3What does this calculator not include?

Two constant rates held for up to 20+ years is a simplification — a real portfolio does not return the same amount every year. Tax, expense ratios and exit loads are not modelled. Education and medical inflation in India often runs higher than general inflation, so for those goals the real number is frequently larger than this estimate.

STEP 4What is a worked example?

At the default settings shown at the top of this page — Child's current age 4 · Marriage at age 25 · Current estimated marriage cost 1000000 · Amount available today 0 · Inflation assumption 6 — this calculator returns ₹2,986 Required monthly SIP · ₹34 L Future cost. Change one input at a time to see which one moves the answer most; that is usually more useful than the single number.

Frequently asked questions

What inflation rate should I assume for a wedding?
Venue and catering costs in Indian cities have often risen faster than general inflation. 6% is the default; try 7–8% as well and plan for the higher figure.
Should I include gold and jewellery in the goal amount?
Yes, include everything you expect to fund. If you plan to buy gold steadily, remember gold does not compound like an investment — treat that portion separately rather than assuming a 12% return on it.
Is it better to save in gold or in mutual funds for this?
Gold is traditional for weddings but volatile and offers no compounding. A mix is common: financial assets for the bulk, gold bought over time for the ceremony itself. The calculator does not recommend one over the other.
My child is already 20 and the wedding is in 5 years. What now?
The monthly figure will be high, and over five years you should favour safer instruments. Consider a shorter-term debt or hybrid allocation rather than equity, because there is little time to recover from a downturn.
