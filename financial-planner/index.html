A: A complete plan includes emergency fund analysis, insurance adequacy check, retirement corpus calculation, SIP/Mutual Fund asset allocation, debt reduction plan, and an instant downloadable PDF report.

What a CFP does that a product seller does not

A Certified Financial Planner is trained to look at your whole position — income, goals, insurance, loans, investments and taxes together — and to write it down as one plan. That is a different job from selling you a policy or a fund, and the difference shows up in what you are told to do.

  • Starts from your goals, not from a product — the amount you invest follows from what you are funding and when.
  • Prices your goals properly — education and retirement are costed with inflation, not at today’s price.
  • Checks protection first — an unprotected family is one emergency away from breaking its plan.
  • Shows the assumptions — you should be able to see every rate and figure used, and challenge it.
  • Reviews on a schedule — a plan written once and never revisited is not a plan.

What to ask before you engage one

  • How are you paid — fee from me, commission from products, or both?
  • Will you give me a written plan that shows your assumptions?
  • Who holds my money, and who can move it?
  • How often will we review, and what happens if my income changes?
  • What is your experience with families in my situation?

Questions people ask

Is MyFinancialPlan a certified financial planner?

No. MyFinancialPlan is a planning platform. It helps you build a complete family financial plan free, and connects you with CFP professionals, investment advisers and wealth managers when you want a human to review or implement it.

What is the difference between a CFP and an investment adviser?

A CFP is a broad planning qualification covering goals, insurance, tax, retirement and estate. An investment adviser focuses on the investment side — asset allocation and portfolio selection. Many people use a planner for the overall plan and an adviser for the portfolio, or one professional who does both.

Do I need to pay for a plan before I know if it is useful?

No. Build the numbers here first — it is free and takes minutes. You will immediately see the goal costs and the monthly amounts they need, which is often enough to start acting on your own.

How often should a financial plan be reviewed?

Once a year as a minimum, and again whenever something material changes — a new child, a job change, a large loan, an inheritance, or a significant market move.

Will a planner push me towards expensive products?

Some are paid by commission and some charge a fee directly. Ask which applies to you before you start, and ask for the answer in writing. That single question removes most of the risk of being sold something unsuitable.
